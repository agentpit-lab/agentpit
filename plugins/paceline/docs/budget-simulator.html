PaceLine budget simulator

pace has no window of its own. It turns your limits into a budget per hour. In limit mode it tells the model the pace in plain words before every Agent and Workflow launch; in inform mode the model reads nothing. Move the inputs and watch that line change.

TL;DRpace spreads what is left of your weekly limit over the rest of the week by the marks of each day's working hours and off-hours, and fixes each part's share at its start. The budget per hour is what is left of the current part's share over its remaining hours. On this page the current part is always on plan. At a hard limit it asks before a launch.

Week plan

Each day has a mark for its working hours (☼) and one for its off-hours (☾): 0 is a day off, 0.5 light, 1 normal, 1.5 heavy. Default: ☼ 1 and ☾ 0 every day. Where the rest of the week's budget goes

Details

How it is computed and what it leaves out

How the budget per hour is computed

At the start of each part, a day's working hours or its off-hours, pace fixes the part's share: what is left of the week, less the reserve, times the part's mark over the sum of the marks of this and every later part. A change of the running part's mark splits it, and the rest starts at once by the same rule. The budget per hour is the share not yet spent over the hours left in the part, capped at what a five-hour window can hold.

What this simulator leaves out

Your usage history (forecast, calibration and the five-hour burn rate), running holds, host memory checks, model-scoped limits, spend inside the current part (taken as 0, so the part is on plan) and the plan gate of limit mode. paceline status and paceline plan on your machine include them.

How the off-hours advice is given

paceline advice returns start, wait or stop with an exit code (0, 10, 11), a budget and an agent count; an unattended run, such as Stint's, obeys it without deciding again.
