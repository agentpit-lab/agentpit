PitBoard row anatomy

What every reading in PitBoard's status row means, how it changes, and what to do when it turns amber or red.

TL;DRGrey: carry on. Amber: worth a glance. Red: change what you do next. On a limit, compare Pace with Budget: above it, the limit runs out early; the tick on the bar marks where an even pace would be now.

    The limit bar

    Three marks on one bar

    The limit bars on the cards and in the /pitboard pane use these three marks. The Desktop row's bar shows used and the tick; the CLI row shows a two-cell gauge.

    Colours

    When a reading turns amber or red

    Thresholds read from PitBoard's code. The 5h reading also turns red when Pace is above Budget, because it then runs out before the reset; with PaceLine's projection it turns red when the window would reach PaceLine's threshold before the reset. The weekly reading changes colour only with % used; inside its card a Pace above Budget is amber, and Overspent is amber, red past a day ahead.

    Details

    Formulas, sources and limits

    How Pace, Budget and At reset are computed

    Pace is the change in usage over the last 90 minutes (five-hour) or 6 hours (week), per hour. Budget is the percent left divided by the hours to the reset. At reset is Used plus Pace times the hours left.

    Where the numbers come from

    Claude Code reports usage to plugins with each model reply. With PaceLine installed, PitBoard also reads PaceLine's limits file, so a fresh session shows the limits another session saw.

    What a cold turn and /compact cost

    A cache write costs 1.25 times the input price with a five-minute TTL and 2 times with a one-hour TTL; a read costs 0.1 times, 0.05 times on Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, and 0.025 times on Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 (Anthropic's prices, prompt caching, read 2026-10-15). With a one-hour TTL a cold turn therefore costs 20, 40 or 80 times a warm one; If cold shows the figure for the session's model. While the cache is warm, /compact reads the history from the cache; after a break longer than the TTL, it processes the whole history uncached (Claude Code, prompt caching, read 2026-10-15). Idle compaction can also summarize a long idle conversation before a one-hour cache expires. How If cold is computed, and how the row shows idle compaction.

    Known compromises

    An idle session can show another session's reading up to about 20 seconds late. A Desktop card's paces are taken at the start of the minute. With every session idle, the limits stop moving unless PaceLine's usage-watch is on.
